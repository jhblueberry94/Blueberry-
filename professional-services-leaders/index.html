Why professional services leaders need LinkedIn now

Professional services firms have long relied on referrals, events and direct relationships. Those channels still work, but the buyers using them have changed how they behave.

Before a general counsel recommends a law firm, before a CFO shortlists an audit partner, before a CEO brings in a consultancy, they research. They look people up and form opinions from what they find. The 6sense 2025 report shows how far that research runs before anyone makes contact.

A partner or director with a credible LinkedIn presence is in a different position during that research. The referral that was always going to happen closes faster, and the new contact who looks you up finds something that confirms the recommendation they just received.

LinkedIn reinforces the relationships that win professional services work and extends them to buyers you have not yet met.

What good LinkedIn content looks like for professional services

The content that builds credibility is specific to your area and grounded in real insight. A corporate lawyer writing about deal dynamics they are seeing. A tax partner explaining what a legislative change means for clients. A consultant setting out a framework for diagnosing a common organisational problem.

That material shows expertise to buyers in the research phase, gives your network something current to share when they refer you, and positions you as a visible voice in your sector. Where the decision is between several credible firms, it can tip the balance.

For professional services leaders, LinkedIn is a reputation channel that makes the work you already do visible to the people who matter.

Common questions about LinkedIn personal branding for professional services leaders

Does LinkedIn work for professional services business development?

Yes, particularly for building credibility with buyers outside your immediate network. Buyers research heavily before they contact a firm, and in Edelman and LinkedIn's 2024 study 73% of buyers rated thought leadership as more trustworthy than marketing materials when judging a company's capabilities.

What should a law firm partner or consulting director post on LinkedIn?

Content grounded in specific expertise and current relevance. Commentary on regulatory changes and what they mean for clients, observations on trends you are seeing, and frameworks for common problems in your sector. Generic content about the firm rarely builds the personal credibility that drives referrals.

Will posting on LinkedIn conflict with client confidentiality?

The content is built around your thinking and publicly available observations, with no client-specific information in it. Anonymised frameworks and sector-level insight show expertise without exposing any client, and you approve every piece before it goes live. Your firm's own policies still apply, and we work within them.

How does this fit around a busy client-facing schedule?

One 45-minute call every two weeks is the only time commitment. We handle the writing, editing, scheduling and day-to-day LinkedIn management, and you review content before anything goes live.

How long does it take to see results in professional services?

Most professional services clients see early signals within 8 to 10 weeks: more relevant profile views, connection requests from target clients and the occasional enquiry. Consistent commercial impact typically builds over 6 to 12 months.
