Why growth leaders face unusually high role volatility

Head of Growth is often the first go-to-market leadership hire at a funded startup, and one of the first roles reshuffled when strategy pivots. Industry hiring analysis of funded B2B SaaS companies suggests the majority of go-to-market leadership hires made after a funding round depart within roughly 18 months, and a first VP of Marketing at a venture-backed company typically lasts under 14 months in the role.

That volatility is structural, not personal. It follows from how fast funded startups change direction. But it means a growth leader whose credibility depends entirely on their current employer's trajectory is exposed to a risk they cannot control from inside the building.

The growth leaders with the most durable careers are not the ones who never got reshuffled. They are the ones with a visible track record that survived the reshuffle.

What good LinkedIn content looks like for a Head of Growth

The content that builds credibility here is specific and falsifiable: an experiment you ran, what the data actually showed, and the framework you used to decide what to test next. Growth leadership is fundamentally accountable for measurable outcomes, and content that demonstrates that discipline reads very differently from generic marketing commentary.

According to Gartner's 2024 research, B2B buyers now complete roughly 80% of their purchase journey through independent, self-directed research, up from 57% in 2015. For a growth leader whose entire discipline is built around understanding buyer behaviour, being visibly good at reading that shift is itself a credibility signal.

For a Head of Growth, LinkedIn content is not brand marketing. It is a public demonstration of the exact analytical discipline the role is supposed to bring.

Common questions

Is a Head of Growth role really volatile enough to justify building a personal brand?

Yes. Growth and GTM leadership roles at funded B2B SaaS companies see high turnover: industry data suggests a large share of go-to-market leadership hires at newly funded startups depart within 18 months of being hired, often when strategy pivots. A visible track record outside any one employer is a genuine hedge against that.

What is the difference between Head of Growth content and CMO content?

Orientation. A CMO typically owns brand and marketing output broadly. A Head of Growth is accountable for measurable growth metrics and experimentation, so credible content here leans into specific frameworks, test results, and data-backed reasoning rather than brand narrative.

What should a Head of Growth post about?

Specific experiments and what they revealed, frameworks you use to prioritise growth bets (AARRR, ICE, RICE, North Star Metric), and honest commentary on what did not work. Generic growth-hacking listicles get scrolled past. Specific, falsifiable claims about what you tested get remembered.

How does this fit around a role that is already highly metrics-driven?

One 45-minute recorded call every two weeks. We ask about the experiments and decisions you are already running, and turn that into content, so it never competes with the analysis work the role actually demands.

How long before this affects how a Head of Growth is perceived externally?

Most see early signals within eight to ten weeks: more relevant profile views, recruiter and investor interest, and inbound from peers. Consistent reputational impact, useful when the role itself carries above-average churn, typically builds over six to twelve months.
