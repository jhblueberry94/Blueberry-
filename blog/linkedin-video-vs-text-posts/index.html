LinkedIn video vs text posts: what B2B founders should post

Text posts are the quicker, easier baseline for a B2B founder, and video earns a higher engagement rate. Buffer's 2026 data puts median video engagement at 7.35% against 3.18% for text. For most founders the best answer is both: regular text posts, plus video clips cut from recorded conversations.

By Josh Huggins · September 2026 · 7 min read

Every founder who starts posting on LinkedIn reaches the same fork: write it, or say it to camera. The LinkedIn video vs text posts decision depends on which job you need a post to do this quarter, and the published data on both formats gives a firmer basis than most advice does.

This post compares the two on engagement, reach, effort and trust. It then sets out when each is the better choice for a B2B founder, consultant or sales leader, and how to run both without doubling your workload.

What the data says about video vs text posts on LinkedIn

Buffer's 2026 analysis of content formats puts the median engagement rate for LinkedIn video at 7.35% and for text-only posts at 3.18%. Socialinsider's benchmark of 1.3 million posts from business pages shows a narrower gap for 2025, with video at 6.00% and text at 4.50%. Both datasets favour video on engagement rate.

Engagement rate is a ratio, so it describes how a post performs among the people who saw it and says nothing about how many people that was. Grow with Ghost's internal tracking of 500 posts reported higher reach for text (2,847 impressions) than for video (1,543), though that is one agency's sample and works better as a pointer than a benchmark.

Metricool's study, covering 63,108 accounts, found that video is now the most-used format on personal profiles and still underperforms carousels, image posts and multi-image posts on most key metrics. More people posting video has not made video the strongest format.

Taken together, the sources suggest that video wins on engagement per view, that text costs far less to produce, and that neither is the top-performing format overall. For a B2B founder the sharper question is what each format does for a buyer who is quietly researching you.

When text posts are the better choice

Text suits an argument. If your idea needs a claim, a reason and an example, three to five paragraphs deliver it faster than a clip, and a reader can take it in between meetings. Independent consultants whose credibility rests on how they think often do their best work in text.

Text also survives a busy week. A post you can draft in ten minutes gets published, while a clip that needs a quiet room, a decent take and an edit often does not. The format you can sustain for six months will outperform the better format you abandon in March.

Choose text while you are still finding your positioning. Writing forces you to state what you think, and the posts that draw a response show you which ideas deserve a spoken version later.

When video is the better choice

Video suits relationships that are about to become conversations. A sales leader or fractional executive who will be on calls with buyers next month gains from being recognised first. The 6sense 2025 B2B Buyer Report found that buyers complete around 61% of their journey before first contact with a vendor. A buyer who has already watched you explain something arrives with a picture of you that written posts rarely provide.

Video also carries emphasis, humour, hesitation and the way you handle a hard question. Those cues are what people use to judge whether they would enjoy working with you.

Keep each clip short and built around one idea, add captions because many viewers watch without sound, and cut the throat-clearing at the start. A B2B founder talking naturally about a client problem for sixty seconds usually beats a scripted piece to camera.

Can you use both? A workable mix for a founder

Most B2B founders do not have to choose. The practical answer is a text baseline with video added for the ideas that work better spoken.

A sensible starting rhythm is two or three text posts a week from your personal profile, plus one clip when you have a story or opinion worth hearing. The two feed each other: a text post that draws a strong response is a good candidate for a clip, and a clip can be rewritten as a text post for the people who never press play.

The workload stays manageable when both come from one recording. A 45-minute conversation about your work gives you material for several written posts and a handful of clips, because the thinking is captured once and shaped into formats afterwards. Where you post matters as well. Company page content tends to reach far fewer people than personal profile content, which is why we recommend publishing from your own profile. Common mistakes when choosing between video and text

The first mistake is reading engagement rate as reach. For example, a clip watched by 400 people who react at a high rate can score better than a text post seen by 4,000, yet the text post put your name in front of ten times as many buyers.

The second is picking a format because someone says the algorithm prefers it. Metricool's data shows video adoption rising while its results lag other formats, so adoption is a poor guide to effectiveness.

The third is judging too early. Neither format shows its value in two weeks. Give each at least a month of regular posting, and compare the conversations it starts as well as the reactions it collects.

The fourth is polishing video until it stops sounding like you. Over-produced clips can look like advertising, and raw unedited ones can wander. A clean cut, captions and a good opening line are enough.
