LinkedIn personal branding case study: 3,600% more impressions in 30 days

In this LinkedIn personal branding case study, an ex-IBM leader with 20 years in corporate leadership went from a dormant profile to a 3,600% rise in impressions in 30 days. One 40-minute recorded conversation produced a three-pillar content plan, his first post went live within 10 days, and targeted outreach added around 100 ICP connections.

By Josh Huggins  ·  November 2026  ·  5 min read

Our client spent 20 years at IBM, moving from manager to senior leader. He now runs his own consultancy as CEO, helping early-stage tech founders turn good products into revenue. He had the experience and a network that already knew him, but his LinkedIn profile showed very little of either.

ClientCEO of a consultancy, 20 years in leadership at IBM

The starting point: a dormant LinkedIn profile

His network had gone quiet. He had built real relationships on LinkedIn in the past, but without regular posting those contacts had stopped seeing him in their feed. There was no content plan, and client work left no time to write one.

He wanted founders and senior decision-makers to see the operator he is, without LinkedIn turning into a second job.

How we rebuilt his LinkedIn presence in 30 days

  1. One 40-minute recorded conversation. We interviewed him about his two decades at IBM, the problems founders bring to him and the views he holds that others in his field don't. That single call became the raw material for his content.
  2. A defined audience and three content pillars. We set out his ideal client profile, then built a content framework around three pillars drawn from his own experience, so every post had a clear job to do.
  3. First post live within 10 days. We wrote and edited everything in his voice, and he approved each piece before it went out.
  4. Outreach alongside the content. We sourced 200 targeted contacts and ran a sequenced LinkedIn outreach campaign, so new connections arrived at a profile that was already active.

What his LinkedIn posts looked like

Every post came from his own stories. One covered his move from manager to leader at IBM, and the habit most new leaders find hardest to drop: the urge to step in and deliver the work themselves. A video post took on the fear many people carry that AI will take their job, and what that means for the people side of a business.

Why content and outreach work better together

In our experience, most people check a sender's profile before accepting a connection request, so an active profile gives them something to judge you on. Edelman's June 2025 research with LinkedIn, The Rise of the Hidden Buyer, found that 95% of hidden buyers (the people who shape a purchase without being your main contact) say strong thought leadership makes them more receptive to sales and marketing outreach.

Timing matters too. Bain & Company noted in June 2026 that around 90% of buyers purchase from their Day 1 list, the providers already in mind before a search begins. Our guide to the Day One shortlist covers how a visible profile gets you onto it.
