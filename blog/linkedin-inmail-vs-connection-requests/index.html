LinkedIn InMail vs connection requests: which gets a response

InMail and connection requests are not interchangeable. Connection requests are free, low-commitment, and generally get a warmer response, while InMail reaches people outside your network but reads more like a sales pitch. The right choice depends on who you are trying to reach and why, not personal preference.

By Josh Huggins · August 2026 · 8 min read

Every B2B founder and sales leader eventually asks the same question: send an InMail, or send a connection request. Most answer it on instinct rather than evidence, which is a shame, because the two tools solve genuinely different problems and perform very differently depending on how they are used.

This is not a small operational detail. Get it wrong repeatedly and you either waste InMail credits on people who were never going to reply to a stranger, or you send generic connection requests to people who needed a proper, targeted approach. Get it right and outreach starts converting at a rate that makes the whole channel worth the effort.

What each one actually is

A connection request is LinkedIn's native, free way to ask someone to join your network. It is limited to a short note, it is available to every user regardless of subscription, and once accepted it opens unlimited messaging with no further restriction.

InMail is a paid feature, included with Premium, Sales Navigator, and Recruiter subscriptions, that lets you message someone directly without being connected. It exists specifically to solve the problem a connection request cannot: reaching someone outside your network who has no reason yet to accept you.

What the data suggests about response rates

Published outreach benchmarks put InMail response rates at 10 to 25%, rising to 30 to 40% for well-targeted, well-written campaigns sent to a genuinely relevant audience. A short, personalised connection request note is commonly reported across outreach benchmarking to be accepted at a noticeably higher rate than an InMail gets a reply, though the exact figure varies significantly by industry, seniority of the recipient, and how targeted the list actually is.

That last stat matters more than it first appears. If most of the decision is already made before contact, the response rate to any individual message depends heavily on whether the recipient has seen you before. Cold InMail to someone who has never encountered you is a genuinely cold approach. A connection request to someone who has already seen three of your posts is not cold at all, whatever the channel.

The mistake most B2B leaders make is not picking the wrong tool. It is using either tool the same generic way regardless of who is on the other end, then blaming the channel when the reply rate is poor.

Why connection requests usually win for relationship-first outreach

A connection request asks for very little: join my network. An InMail asks for much more: read this, trust this stranger, and reply, all in one step, often while it visibly cost the sender a credit to send. That difference in perceived commitment shows up in how each is received. Once a connection request is accepted, the relationship is also structurally different. Messaging is unlimited and free, and the recipient can see your profile, your posts, and your activity going forward. InMail typically allows one follow-up if the first message goes unanswered, and if it fails, the conversation is usually over.

This is why, for founders and consultants building relationship-first pipelines rather than running high-volume outbound campaigns, connection requests backed by a genuine LinkedIn presence tend to outperform InMail on both cost and long-term relationship quality. It is also consistent with how independent consultants and fractional executives actually win work: through visibility and warm familiarity building over time, not through a single well-crafted paid message landing at the right moment.

The variable that changes everything: are you visible before you reach out

Neither InMail nor connection requests perform consistently well in isolation from what a prospect already knows about you. A message from someone with an active, credible LinkedIn presence is read completely differently to the identical message from someone whose profile shows no activity. This is the part most outreach advice skips. The tool matters less than the context the recipient brings to reading it. Sales leaders and founders who post consistently are not just building brand awareness in the abstract. They are quietly raising the response rate of every message they will ever send, on either channel, before they have sent a single one.

A practical rule of thumb

Default to a personalised connection request for everyday B2B prospecting. Reserve InMail for a shortlist of specific, high-value targets outside your network where you have a genuinely relevant, well-researched reason to reach out, and where the cost per message is easily justified by the size of the opportunity. Spend more time on your own visibility than on either message template. It is the one factor that improves both channels at once.
