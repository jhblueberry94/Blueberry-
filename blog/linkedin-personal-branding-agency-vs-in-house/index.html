LinkedIn personal branding agency vs in-house hire: cost, speed and fit

An in-house hire costs roughly £52,500 a year at the UK average social media salary once employer National Insurance is added, while a specialist agency starts from £12,000 a year. The better choice depends on how many leaders need a personal brand, how much wider marketing work you have, and how much of your own time you can give.

By Josh Huggins · October 2026 · 8 min read

Founders and senior leaders who decide to build a LinkedIn personal brand reach the same fork: hire someone, or hire a specialist. Both work. They suit different situations, and the cost gap is smaller than most people expect once employment costs are counted.

This guide compares the two on cost, speed, expertise, time and risk, and says when each is the better choice. We are an agency, so we have tried to say plainly where an in-house hire wins.

What each option is

An in-house hire is an employee, usually a content or social media manager, who writes and publishes for you and is managed by you or your marketing lead. A specialist agency is an outside team that interviews you, writes, schedules, engages and reports. A freelance ghostwriter sits between the two, and we cover that route in personal branding vs ghostwriting on LinkedIn.

What an in-house hire costs in the UK

Digital Waffle's 2026 UK Social Media Salary Guide puts the average salary for social media roles at £46,267. The guide uses 2025 data and blends several role types, so treat it as a guide rather than a quote. Employers also pay 15% National Insurance on earnings above £5,000 a year (gov.uk rates for 2026 to 2027), which adds about £6,190 at that salary and brings the cost to roughly £52,500. That is before pension contributions, recruitment fees, software, equipment, training and management time.

At the entry level it costs less than a quarter of the hire.

Speed, quality and the part neither option can skip

Either route depends on the same thing: access to your thinking. A writer, whether employed or external, can only turn into posts what they can get out of you. That is why our process starts with a recorded conversation, and why an in-house hire needs a similar routine to produce anything specific.

It also explains why speed to the first post is rarely the deciding factor. A personal brand builds through posting consistently over months, so the option that keeps producing through holidays and busy quarters tends to serve you better than one that starts fast and stalls. Our guide to whether a LinkedIn personal brand pays off covers the timescales.

When an in-house hire is the better choice

Hiring in-house wins in a few clear situations:

  • You need content for several leaders, plus the company page, at a daily pace.
  • The role covers wider marketing, such as email, events, design and employer branding.
  • You want someone who can react to news within the hour and sit in internal meetings.
  • You have the budget for a full salary and a manager with time to guide the hire.
  • Your subject needs daily internal context that is hard to hand over in a call.

When an agency is the better choice

An agency tends to fit when:

  • One leader, or a small group, needs a personal brand and you want to start without recruiting.
  • You want a tested process, including interviews, editing and reporting, from the first week.
  • Your time is short and one call every two weeks is what you can give.
  • You want to test the channel before committing to a salary, and a 90-day sprint is a smaller step than a permanent hire.
  • You want cover for holidays and sickness without a gap in posting.

A hybrid that often works

The two are not exclusive. An in-house marketer can run the company page, events and email while an agency handles the founder's personal brand. Our comparison of personal profile vs company page explains why the two channels do different jobs. How to decide

  1. How many leaders need a personal brand? One or two points towards an agency. A whole leadership team plus the company page points towards in-house.
  2. How much wider marketing work sits around it? A lot of it points towards in-house.
  3. How much of your own time can you give? One call every two weeks is enough for an agency, and an in-house hire needs more regular guidance.
  4. How sure are you about the channel? If you want to test it first, a 90-day sprint is a smaller commitment than a permanent hire.
