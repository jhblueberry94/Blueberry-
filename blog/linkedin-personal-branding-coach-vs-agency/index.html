LinkedIn personal branding coach vs agency: which should a B2B leader hire

Choose a personal branding coach if you have several hours a week to write and post your own LinkedIn content and want to build the skill yourself. Choose an agency if you want the content produced from a short recorded conversation, with your time limited to that call and a quick approval.

By Josh Huggins · October 2026 · 6 min read

Most B2B leaders who decide to build a LinkedIn presence end up weighing two kinds of help: a personal branding coach who teaches you to do it yourself, or an agency that produces the content with you. Blueberry Media is an agency, so read this knowing where we stand. We have kept the comparison to points you can check with any provider.

Personal branding coach vs agency: the comparison

The main difference is who does the work. With a coach you produce the content yourself, and with an agency a team produces it from your thinking.

What a LinkedIn personal branding coach does

A coach helps you clarify your positioning, shape your profile, plan topics and improve your writing. Good coaching leaves you with a repeatable method and the confidence to run it without help.

Coaching builds skill, and the writing stays with you. You still need to find the hours each week to draft, post and reply, which is where programmes tend to stall for leaders with a full diary.

What a LinkedIn personal branding agency does

An agency takes on the production. In a voice-led model, you have a recorded conversation and the team turns it into posts, clips and newsletters for you to approve. Our guide to LinkedIn personal branding agencies in the UK compares nine of them, including us.

The risk to watch is voice. Some agencies and freelance ghostwriters work from a short written brief, which can produce competent, generic posts. Ask how they capture your thinking before they write a word. We cover that distinction in personal branding vs ghostwriting on LinkedIn.

How much of your time each option takes

Writing three or four LinkedIn posts a week yourself takes four to six hours once ideas, drafting and replies are counted, by the estimate in our guide to done-for-you LinkedIn content. Coaching improves what you produce in those hours without reducing them. An agency model built on a recorded conversation brings your time down to the call and a short approval step. Ours is a 45-minute call every two weeks.

For most leaders, time settles the choice. If you have the hours and enjoy writing, a coach is good value. If your diary is already full, the posts tend to go unwritten however good the coaching is.

Gaps in posting have a cost. Bain & Company research published in June 2026 found that around 90% of B2B buyers purchase from their Day 1 list, the providers they already have in mind when the buying process starts. The months you spend silent are months when buyers are forming that list without seeing you.

What each option costs in the UK

Done-for-you LinkedIn content from a UK agency costs between £1,000 and £6,000 a month. Entry-level services covering three or four posts a week start at around £1,000 to £1,500. Full-service packages with strategy, video clips, newsletters and reporting run from £3,000 to £6,000.

Coaching is usually priced per session or per programme. Published UK examples in October 2026 range from a few hundred pounds for a single one-to-one session to around £3,000 for a 12-week programme. Add the value of your own writing time before you compare, because with a coach you are still the person producing the content.

When a coach is the better choice

  • You enjoy writing and want to build the skill yourself.
  • You can protect four to six hours a week for LinkedIn.
  • You are an independent consultant with a small audience and want to find your voice before increasing your output.

When an agency is the better choice

  • Your calendar is full and writing keeps slipping to next week.
  • You lead a team and want consistent output without managing a freelancer.
  • You are a B2B founder, a CEO or a fractional executive who needs to stay visible while you serve clients.

Can you use a coach and an agency together?

Yes, and many leaders do so in sequence. A short period of coaching can sharpen your positioning and themes, after which an agency can handle production. The reverse also works: an agency produces the content and you handle the comments yourself, which is the part of LinkedIn that builds relationships fastest.

Questions to ask a coach or an agency before you hire

  1. Who writes the content, and how will they learn my voice?
  2. How many hours will this need from me each month?
  3. What is the minimum contract length, and what happens if it is not working?
  4. What do you report, and does any of it relate to enquiries or revenue?
  5. Who else do you work with in my sector?
