B2B buyer intent signals on LinkedIn: what to watch for before you reach out

B2B buyer intent signals on LinkedIn are the behavioural cues, such as profile visits, content engagement patterns, and role-relevant trigger events, that show a prospect is actively evaluating a purchase before they make contact. Most of this activity happens silently: 61% of the buying journey is complete before a vendor is contacted.

By Josh Huggins · August 2026 · 8 min read

Most B2B sales teams still treat LinkedIn as a place to send messages, not a place to read them. That is backwards. By the time a buyer is ready for a conversation, they have already told you a great deal about how ready they are, if you know what to watch for.

According to the 6sense 2025 B2B Buyer Report, buyers are 61% of the way through their purchase decision before they make first contact with any vendor. During that stretch, they are not invisible. They are visiting profiles, reading posts, and quietly forming a shortlist. The signals are there. Most vendors just are not watching.

What a real intent signal looks like

The mistake most sales teams make is treating any engagement as equal. A stray like from someone scrolling on their commute is not the same as a pattern of behaviour from a named decision-maker at a target account. Real buyer intent signals cluster, and they usually involve more than one action from the same person over a short window.

Profile-level signals

Repeated visits to your LinkedIn profile from the same person, especially someone in a buying-relevant role, is one of the clearest available signals. A single visit means little. Three or more over a fortnight, particularly from someone whose title matches your ICP, is worth noting.

Content-engagement signals

Comments carry more weight than likes, and comments on a specific, problem-focused post carry more weight than a comment on a general one. Someone who engages with a post about a cost you help solve is telling you something a like never will.

Account-level signals

A single engaged individual is useful. A cluster of engagement from several people at the same company, especially across different seniority levels, is a far stronger signal. Given that buying groups now average 10 or more people, account-level clustering is often more predictive than any one person's behaviour.

Trigger-event signals

A new senior hire in a relevant function, a funding announcement, or a public comment about a problem your service solves are all trigger events. They do not guarantee readiness, but they materially raise the odds that outreach lands at a useful moment rather than a random one.

Why most vendors miss these signals entirely

Buying behaviour research consistently describes a "silent phase": buyers who read your content, view your profile, and evaluate your company without liking, commenting, or connecting. That phase is by design. Raising your hand too early exposes a buyer to sales pressure before they are ready for it, so most of them simply do not.

This is precisely why content matters as much as monitoring. A buyer who has nothing of yours to engage with cannot leave a signal, silent or otherwise. Consistent posting from a named individual, not a company page, gives buyers something to react to, and every reaction becomes a data point you would not otherwise see. This is the same mechanism covered in our piece on B2B buying behaviour on LinkedIn, and it is why founders who post regularly report inbound interest that seems to arrive "out of nowhere." It rarely is. It is the visible tip of research that has been happening for weeks.

How to act on a signal without sounding like a robot

Spotting a signal is only useful if the follow-up does not undo it. A message sent the moment someone views your profile reads as automated, even when a human sent it. The better approach is to let two or three signals accumulate, then reference something specific and genuine rather than opening with a pitch.

Signals versus software

Paid intent-data platforms score accounts using far more data than any individual can track manually, and for larger sales teams they are worth the investment. But for a founder-led B2B business, native LinkedIn analytics (profile viewers, post engagement, and connection requests) cover the signals that matter most. The discipline of checking them consistently matters more than the sophistication of the tool.
