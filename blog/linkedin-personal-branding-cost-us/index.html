LinkedIn personal branding cost in the US: what founders and executives pay in 2026

LinkedIn personal branding in the US typically costs $500 to $3,000 a month with a freelance writer, $3,500 to $5,000 with a boutique LinkedIn-only agency, and $8,000 to $25,000 with mid-market and full-service firms, based on 2026 pricing guides from Clash and 1DS Collective. Blueberry Media, a UK agency serving US clients, starts from £1,000 a month (about $1,325).

By Josh Huggins · October 2026 · 7 min read

LinkedIn personal branding cost in the US varies more than most founders expect, because the same label covers a freelance writer, a coach, a LinkedIn specialist and a full-service PR firm. This guide sets out what each option costs in dollars, what the fee buys, and how to compare quotes.

A note on bias: Blueberry Media is a UK-based LinkedIn personal branding agency that works with US clients, and our own price is in the table. Every other figure comes from a named, dated pricing guide, linked where it appears.

Why the price ranges are so wide

Four things move the price more than anything else.

  • Channels. A LinkedIn-only service costs less than one that also covers a website, a newsletter, podcasts and press.
  • Production. Filmed video, design and photography add cost quickly. Clash lists a founder photoshoot at $3,000 to $7,500 on its own.
  • Media and speaking. Firms that book interviews and stages often charge a retainer plus commission.
  • City. Clash reports boutique LinkedIn-only work at $4,500 to $5,500 a month in New York and the Bay Area, against $3,500 to $5,000 nationally.

Who does the thinking matters too. A writer working from a brief costs less than a team that interviews you, shapes your positioning and decides what you should say. If you choose the cheaper route, you supply the ideas.

What sits inside a monthly fee

Strategy and positioning

Most agencies start with a positioning phase: who you are writing for, what you want to be known for, and which topics you can speak on with authority. 1DS Collective says strategy-phase fees run $2,000 to $15,000 in the proposals it sees, billed before the retainer begins. Ask whether it is included in the monthly fee or billed separately.

Writing and production

This is the bulk of the cost. Ask how many posts, articles or clips you get each month, and how the agency learns your views. Content written from a brief tends to sound generic, while content built from your own spoken answers sounds like you.

Profile work

Your profile is where every post sends people. Clash lists a standalone LinkedIn profile rebrand at $1,500 to $3,500. Many retainers include it in the first month, so check before you pay twice.

Publishing, engagement and reporting

Some services post for you and reply to comments, while others hand over drafts. Monthly reporting should name the people engaging as well as the totals.

How to judge what the fee buys you

The audience is large and the buyers are reading. LinkedIn reports more than 290 million members across North America, a figure that covers the US and Canada. Edelman's June 2025 article on its research with LinkedIn, The Rise of the Hidden Buyer, reports that 95% of hidden buyers say strong thought leadership makes them more receptive to sales and marketing outreach. Forrester's January 2026 2026 State of Business Buying release puts the typical buying decision at 13 internal stakeholders and nine external influencers, so more than one person is likely to read what you publish.

So the useful test is what a provider's work does with the people you want to reach. Ask every option the same four questions:

  1. Whose words end up in the posts, and how do you capture them?
  2. How many hours a month will this take from me?
  3. What is the minimum term, and what notice do I give to stop?
  4. What do you report, and does any of it relate to pipeline?

If you are still deciding between learning it yourself and handing it over, our comparison of a LinkedIn personal branding coach vs agency covers the time each option takes.

Hiring a UK agency from the US

Personal branding work runs over video calls and shared drafts, so the agency's location matters less than its process. Three practical points are worth checking before you sign with any provider outside the US.

  • Currency. A price set in pounds moves with the exchange rate. Ask which currency you will be billed in.
  • Time zones. You need one regular slot that suits both sides, not daily meetings.
  • Voice. US audiences tend to expect a more direct tone. Our post on personal branding in the UK vs the US explains the differences.

Which option fits which leader

A DIY tool suits you if you enjoy writing and have a few hours a week. A coach suits you if you want to build the skill yourself. A freelance writer suits you if you already know what you want to say and need it drafted.

An agency suits you when time is the constraint. That is usually the case for B2B founders, SaaS founders and fractional executives, whose client work comes first.
