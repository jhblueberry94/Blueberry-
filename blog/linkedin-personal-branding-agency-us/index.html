Choosing a LinkedIn personal branding agency in the US: 7 options compared

The right LinkedIn personal branding agency in the US depends on what you need. Full-service PR firms suit leaders who want media and speaking, executive communications agencies suit enterprise teams, and LinkedIn specialists such as Blueberry Media (UK-based) suit founders who want regular posts in their own voice. Compare each agency's model, minimum term and published pricing first.

By Josh Huggins · October 2026 · 7 min read

Choosing a LinkedIn personal branding agency in the US is harder than it looks, because firms with very different models use the same words. A PR firm that books you on podcasts, a social media agency that runs your inbox and a LinkedIn specialist that writes your posts can all call themselves personal branding agencies.

This guide compares seven options: five US-based firms and two UK firms that serve US clients. One of them is Blueberry Media, so read it with that bias in mind. Each agency's details come from its own website or from a named source, linked in the table, and checked in October 2026.

The four types of agency you will meet

Full-service personal brand and PR firms

These firms combine content with press coverage, speaking slots and sometimes awards or profiles on other platforms. They suit leaders who want a public profile beyond LinkedIn and have the budget for it. LinkedIn is one channel among several, so ask how much of the fee goes to it.

Executive communications agencies

These agencies sit inside a wider marketing or PR relationship with a company. They suit enterprise teams where the CEO's posts need to align with corporate messaging, legal review and a communications calendar.

Social and LinkedIn management agencies

These focus on activity: posting, engagement, connection growth and messaging. They suit sales teams that want more conversations. Check whether the service writes your views or mainly runs outreach on your behalf.

LinkedIn personal branding specialists

These focus on one thing: a steady presence on LinkedIn that sounds like you. They suit B2B founders, independent consultants and fractional executives who sell on their own credibility and have little time to write.

Coaching firms sit beside all four. A coach teaches you to do the work yourself, which suits leaders who enjoy writing and can protect the hours.

Why the choice matters for B2B leaders

The people who influence a purchase are reading. Edelman's June 2025 article The Rise of the Hidden Buyer, drawing on its 2025 B2B Thought Leadership Impact Report with LinkedIn, reports that 64% of hidden buyers trust thought leadership more than product sheets or brochures when assessing capabilities. Bain & Company's June 2026 article Raising the Odds on a Deal reports that around 90% of buyers purchase from their Day 1 list, citing earlier research rather than its own survey. And LinkedIn reports more than 290 million members across North America.

An agency whose model does not match your goal costs you more than its fee. A founder who wants buyers to recognise their thinking gains little from a service built around connection volume, and a CEO who needs press coverage will outgrow a LinkedIn-only writer.

Questions to ask before you sign

  1. Who writes the content, and how do they learn my views? A brief produces generic posts. A recorded conversation or interview produces your own language.
  2. How many hours a month will this take from me? Get a number, not a range.
  3. What is the minimum term, and what notice do I give? Ask before you see the proposal.
  4. What do you report? Look for who is engaging and whether any of it connects to pipeline.
  5. Where is the team, and who will I speak to each month? Check the agency's own website for its address, as some widely shared "best agency" lists contain errors.
