Reading, Berkshire

Why LinkedIn matters for B2B founders in Reading

Reading is one of the strongest business economies in the UK. In the Centre for Cities' Cities Outlook 2026, which compares the UK's 63 largest cities and towns, Reading ranks fourth for businesses per head of population and fifth for new economy firms, the knowledge-intensive businesses working at the edge of new technology. Its average weekly workplace earnings of £917 are the third highest in the country, behind only London and Cambridge.

That density works for and against a founder. Your buyers are close by and spend their working day online, and so do plenty of your competitors. Enterprise buyers also decide in groups. Forrester's State of Business Buying 2026 found the typical B2B buying decision involves 13 internal stakeholders and nine external influencers. Most of those people will check you out before anyone gets in touch, and LinkedIn is where a founder's thinking is easiest to find.

A consistent LinkedIn presence means that when someone at a Green Park or Thames Valley Park employer looks you up, they find recent, specific posts from you rather than a profile that went quiet two years ago. Our guide to the Day One shortlist explains why being known before the first call matters so much.

Where Reading's B2B buyers work

Reading's business community is spread across a few large business parks and a busy town centre. Each has a different mix of employers, and each shapes who you are likely to be selling to.

Thames Valley Park

On Reading's eastern edge beside the Thames, Thames Valley Park is where Microsoft has its UK headquarters, and it has been home to Oracle for decades. The park sits just over the boundary in Wokingham borough, so buyers there often think of themselves as part of both places.

Green Park

Beside junction 11 of the M4, Green Park is home to Huawei's UK headquarters and to occupiers including PepsiCo, Bayer and Thales. Several of the businesses here are UK or European bases of global groups, which means long buying cycles and large buying groups.

The town centre

Reading's town centre has its own office core around Forbury Place and the station, with law, accountancy and advisory firms alongside tech and life sciences businesses. Direct Elizabeth line services have linked Reading to central London since November 2022, which puts London buyers within easy reach too.

Thames Valley Science Park

South of the M4 at Shinfield, the University of Reading's science park hosts growth businesses alongside Shinfield Studios, a film and TV studio complex. It brings research-led founders and creative sector suppliers into the local buyer mix.

Knight Frank's 2024 research on Reading found that technology, media and telecoms firms made up 40% of the town's office take-up over the previous five years, while pharmaceutical, medical and healthcare businesses led activity in 2023.

Who we work with in Reading

The service suits B2B founders, consultants and senior leaders whose buyers sit in or around these organisations. In Reading, that tends to mean:

  • founders of software and IT services businesses selling into enterprise teams (see LinkedIn for SaaS founders)
  • independent consultants and advisers who win work through reputation and referral (see LinkedIn for consultants)
  • partners and directors at law, accountancy and advisory firms (see LinkedIn for professional services leaders)
  • leaders in life sciences and healthcare businesses
  • specialist suppliers to the film and creative businesses growing around Shinfield

What they have in common is expertise that buyers cannot see yet. Their reputation lives in referrals and client conversations, and their LinkedIn profile says very little about it.

How the service works

Every two weeks, we record a 45-minute conversation with you. We send the questions in advance, and you talk about your market, your experience and what you are seeing right now. From that conversation, we write three to four LinkedIn posts a week in your voice, because the material comes straight from you. Nothing goes live until you approve it.

Every package includes profile optimisation, a content strategy built around your commercial goals, and a monthly report on reach, profile views, connection growth and inbound conversations. Higher-tier packages add newsletter writing, outreach management and short video clips from your recorded conversation.

The service runs remotely, so you never need to travel to us. We are based in Hungerford, further west along the M4 in Berkshire, and know the Thames Valley business community well. Every engagement starts with an initial 90-day sprint, then moves to monthly rolling with 30 days notice. See our LinkedIn personal branding agency page for prices and the full breakdown.

How you will know it is working

We report monthly on three things: whether your posts reach people who match your buyers, whether people at target accounts start to recognise you, and the conversations that follow. Reach usually moves first. Enquiries and warmer conversations typically follow at around 60 to 90 days. Our guide to how to measure LinkedIn personal branding explains each measure in detail.
